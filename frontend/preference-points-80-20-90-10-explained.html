Preference points explained: 80/20 and 90/10

Government tenders are scored on price and on preference points. Knowing how the scoring works helps you price and prepare smarter.

The basic idea

Under the Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act (PPPFA) and its regulations, a bid is scored on price and on points for “specific goals” set out in the tender, such as ownership by historically disadvantaged people, women, youth or people with disabilities, or location. You claim these points on form SBD 6.1 and back them up with proof.

The two systems

  • 80/20: 80 points for price and 20 for specific goals. Used for tenders from R30,000 up to R50 million.
  • 90/10: 90 points for price and 10 for specific goals. Used above R50 million.

The buyer states which system applies in the tender document. Under these systems, the lowest compliant price earns the most price points, so a strong price still matters most.

What is changing

The Public Procurement Act, 2024 was signed into law in July 2024 but, as of the last time we reviewed it, has not yet come into force. National Treasury published draft regulations for comment in April 2026. Until the Act commences, the PPPFA and the 2022 regulations continue to apply. This page is general information and is not legal advice. Always follow the rules stated in each tender document and check National Treasury for the latest position.

How to use this

  • Read the evaluation criteria before you price.
  • Claim only points you can prove, and attach the proof the tender lists.
  • Keep your certificates and affidavits current. See the tender documents checklist.
