Tender documents checklist (and the SBD forms explained)

Bids are often rejected for missing paperwork, not for a bad price. Use this checklist, then always check the specific bid document.

Company and compliance documents

  • CSD registration report (and your supplier number)
  • Tax compliance status (TCS) pin or letter from SARS
  • Company registration documents from CIPC and a list of directors
  • B-BBEE certificate or sworn affidavit, if you claim preference points
  • Bank confirmation letter
  • COIDA letter of good standing, where required
  • Industry registrations where relevant: CIDB for construction, PSIRA for security, and similar

The standard bidding documents (SBD forms)

Many government tenders include standard forms. The ones you meet most often are:

  • SBD 1: the invitation to bid, with your details and the declaration that you have read the conditions.
  • SBD 3.1 / 3.2 / 3.3: the pricing schedules (firm prices, non-firm prices, or professional services).
  • SBD 4: a declaration of interest, so the buyer knows if you are connected to anyone in the department.
  • SBD 6.1: the preference points claim form, where you claim points for specific goals.
  • SBD 8: a declaration about past supply chain practices.
  • SBD 9: a certificate that you did not collude with other bidders.

How to avoid disqualification

  • Fill in every field, sign every page where asked, and use the original forms from the bid pack.
  • Attach certified copies only if the bid asks for them, and check how recent they must be.
  • Submit at the right place and before the closing time. Late bids are not considered.
